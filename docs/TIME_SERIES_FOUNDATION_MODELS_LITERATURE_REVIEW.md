## Abstract

Over the past seven decades, the discipline of time series analysis has undergone fundamental paradigm shifts: from classical stochastic difference equations and spectral representations, through state-space Kalman filtering and deep recurrent sequence models, to the modern era of **Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs)** pre-trained across hundreds of billions of observations. 

This literature review provides an exhaustive, self-contained mathematical synthesis of this evolution. Starting from the measure-theoretic probability foundations of stochastic processes, stationary representations, and Wold's Decomposition Theorem, we systematically trace the architectural mechanics that enabled universal zero-shot and few-shot temporal forecasting. We examine the mathematical failure modes of naive point-wise transformers, the continuous patching revolution that yielded $\mathcal{O}(P^2)$ computational speedups, and the contemporary breakthrough of In-Context Fine-Tuning (TimesFM-ICF, ICML 2025) utilizing learnable Common Separator Tokens ($\text{[SEP]}$) to achieve supervised fine-tuning accuracy with zero parameter updates. Finally, we establish the bridge to dynamical systems theory, illustrating how Takens' Delay Embedding Theorem and the Lyapunov attractor spectrum (Kuramoto, Stuart–Landau, and Kuramoto–Sivashinsky) provide the mathematical ground truth to verify whether foundation models recover true physical invariants.

## 1. Mathematical Foundations of Stochastic Processes

### 1.1 The Probability Space and Temporal Indexing
Let $ be a complete probability space, where:
- $\Omega$ denotes the sample space of elementary outcomes $\omega \in \Omega$,
- $\mathcal{F} \subseteq 2^\Omega$ is a $\sigma569Xalgebra of measurable events, and
- : \mathcal{F} \to [0, 1]$ is a countably additive probability measure with (\Omega) = 1$.

A **stochastic process** is a parameterized collection of random variables indexed by a totally ordered temporal index set $\mathcal{T} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$:
29832\mathbf{X} = \{ X_t(\omega) : t \in \mathcal{T}, \omega \in \Omega \}29832
For discrete-time systems, $\mathcal{T} = \mathbb{Z}$ or $\mathcal{T} = \{1, 2, \dots, T\}$. For a fixed $\omega \in \Omega$, the realization  \mapsto X_t(\omega)$ defines a deterministic trajectory or sample path.

### 1.2 Filtrations and the Prediction Problem
Let $\mathbb{F} = \{\mathcal{F}_t\}_{t \in \mathcal{T}}$ define a filtration—an increasing family of sub-$\sigma569Xalgebras such that $\mathcal{F}_s \subseteq \mathcal{F}_t \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ for all  \le t$. The natural filtration generated by the process history up to time $ is:
29832\mathcal{F}_t = \sigma(\{ X_s : s \le t \})29832

The fundamental objective of statistical forecasting is to compute the optimal 569Xstep-ahead predictor $\hat{X}_{t+h}$ under a chosen risk function. In Hilbert space ^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ endowed with the inner product $\langle X, Y \rangle = \mathbb{E}[XY]$, the minimum mean squared error (MMSE) predictor is given by the orthogonal projection:
29832\hat{X}_{t+h} = \operatorname{argmin}_{Y \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}_t, P)} \mathbb{E}[(X_{t+h} - Y)^2] = \mathbb{E}[X_{t+h} \mid \mathcal{F}_t]29832

### 1.3 Stationarity Classes
Stationarity provides the mathematical invariance necessary to infer population properties from a single finite trajectory realization.

#### Definition 1.1 (Strict Stationarity)
A process $\{X_t\}$ is **strictly (strongly) stationary** if the joint distribution of any finite collection of coordinates is invariant under arbitrary temporal shifts:
29832F_{X_{t_1}, \dots, X_{t_k}}(x_1, \dots, x_k) = F_{X_{t_1 + \tau}, \dots, X_{t_k + \tau}}(x_1, \dots, x_k) \quad \forall k \in \mathbb{N}, \; t_i, \tau \in \mathcal{T}29832

#### Definition 1.2 (Wide-Sense / Weak Stationarity)
A process $\{X_t\}$ with finite second moments ($\mathbb{E}[X_t^2] < \infty$) is **weakly (covariance) stationary** if:
1. The mean is temporally invariant:
   29832\mathbb{E}[X_t] = \mu \quad \forall t \in \mathcal{T}29832
2. The autocovariance function depends solely on the temporal lag  = |s - t|$:
   29832\gamma(s, t) = \operatorname{Cov}(X_s, X_t) = \mathbb{E}[(X_s - \mu)(X_t - \mu)] = \gamma(s - t) = \gamma(k)29832

The **Autocorrelation Function (ACF)** $\rho(k)$ is the normalized dimensionless ratio:
29832\rho(k) = \frac{\gamma(k)}{\gamma(0)} \in [-1, 1], \quad \rho(0) = 129832

The **Partial Autocorrelation Function (PACF)** $\alpha(k)$ measures the conditional correlation between $ and {t-k}$ after linearly eliminating the intermediate intervening variables $\{X_{t-1}, \dots, X_{t-k+1}\}$:
29832\alpha(k) = \operatorname{Corr}(X_t - \hat{\mathcal{P}}_{t-1, k}X_t, \; X_{t-k} - \hat{\mathcal{P}}_{t-1, k}X_{t-k})29832
where $\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{t-1, k}$ denotes orthogonal projection onto $\operatorname{span}\{X_{t-1}, \dots, X_{t-k+1}\}$.

## 2. Classical Representations & Decomposition Theory

### 2.1 Wold's Decomposition Theorem
The foundational bedrock of linear time series analysis was proven by Herman Wold in 1938. It establishes that every discrete-time covariance-stationary process can be uniquely separated into two mutually orthogonal components: a purely indeterministic moving-average process and a singular deterministic process.

#### Theorem 2.1 (Wold Decomposition Theorem)
Any weakly stationary process $\{X_t\}$ with $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = 0$ can be uniquely expressed as:
29832X_t = \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \psi_j \epsilon_{t-j} + V_t29832
satisfying:
1. $\psi_0 = 1$ and the coefficients are square-summable: $\sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \psi_j^2 < \infty$,
2. $\{\epsilon_t\}$ is a white-noise innovation process: $\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_t] = 0$, $\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_t^2] = \sigma^2$, and $\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_t \epsilon_s] = 0$ for  \ne s$,
3. $\epsilon_t \in \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{X_s : s \le t\}$ (the innovations are causal),
4. $\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_t V_s] = 0$ for all , s$ (orthogonality),
5. $\{V_t\}$ is deterministic:  \in \overline{\operatorname{span}}\{V_s : s \le t - 1\}$ for all $.

*Significance for Modern Foundation Models:* Wold's theorem guarantees that any stationary series can be approximated arbitrarily well by an infinite linear filter of innovations. Modern neural foundation models essentially generalize Wold's theorem to nonlinear, deep representations with multi-scale learned filter banks.

### 2.2 The Linear ARMA Family
Using the backward shift (lag) operator $\mathcal{B}$ defined by $\mathcal{B}^k X_t = X_{t-k}$:

#### Autoregressive Process $\text{AR}(p)$:
29832\Phi(\mathcal{B}) X_t = \epsilon_t, \quad \Phi(z) = 1 - \sum_{i=1}^p \phi_i z^i29832
**Stationarity Condition:** All roots of the characteristic polynomial $\Phi(z) = 0$ must lie strictly outside the complex unit circle ($\{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| > 1\}$).

#### Moving Average Process $\text{MA}(q)$:
29832X_t = \Theta(\mathcal{B}) \epsilon_t, \quad \Theta(z) = 1 + \sum_{j=1}^q \theta_j z^j29832
**Invertibility Condition:** All roots of $\Theta(z) = 0$ must lie strictly outside the complex unit circle, permitting representation as an $\text{AR}(\infty)$.

#### Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average $\text{ARIMA}(p, d, q)$:
When a process exhibits polynomial non-stationarity, difference operator $\nabla = (1 - \mathcal{B})$ is applied $ times:
29832\Phi(\mathcal{B}) (1 - \mathcal{B})^d X_t = \Theta(\mathcal{B}) \epsilon_t29832

### 2.3 Spectral Representation & Herglotz's Theorem
By Herglotz's theorem, the autocovariance function $\gamma(k)$ of any weakly stationary process admits a Fourier-Stieltjes integral representation:
29832\gamma(k) = \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} e^{ik\lambda} dF(\lambda)29832
where (\lambda)$ is the spectral distribution function. When $ is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure, (\lambda) = f(\lambda) d\lambda$, where (\lambda)$ is the **spectral density**:
29832f(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} \gamma(k) e^{-ik\lambda}, \quad \lambda \in [-\pi, \pi]29832
The spectral density illuminates the frequency composition of temporal signals, explaining why transformer attention heads act as learned adaptive bandpass filters.

## 3. State-Space Formulations & The Kalman Filter

### 3.1 Linear Gaussian State-Space Models (LGSSM)
State-space models separate the hidden physical/latent state  \in \mathbb{R}^m$ from the observed noisy measurements  \in \mathbb{R}^d$:

29832\begin{aligned}
h_t &= F_t h_{t-1} + G_t \eta_t, \quad \eta_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, Q_t) \quad \text{(Transition Equation)} \\
y_t &= H_t h_t + \epsilon_t, \quad \epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R_t) \quad \text{(Observation Equation)}
\end{aligned}29832

where noise vectors $\eta_t$ and $\epsilon_t$ are mutually independent across all time horizons.

### 3.2 The Kalman Filter Recursion
The Kalman Filter calculates the exact posterior marginals $\mathcal{N}(\hat{h}_{t|t}, P_{t|t})$ using a recursive predictor-corrector cycle:

1. **Time Update (Predict):**
   29832\begin{aligned}
   \hat{h}_{t|t-1} &= F_t \hat{h}_{t-1|t-1} \\
   P_{t|t-1} &= F_t P_{t-1|t-1} F_t^T + G_t Q_t G_t^T
   \end{aligned}29832

2. **Measurement Update (Correct):**
   29832\begin{aligned}
   \tilde{y}_t &= y_t - H_t \hat{h}_{t|t-1} \quad \text{(Innovation / Residual)} \\
   S_t &= H_t P_{t|t-1} H_t^T + R_t \quad \text{(Innovation Covariance)} \\
   K_t &= P_{t|t-1} H_t^T S_t^{-1} \quad \text{(Optimal Kalman Gain)} \\
   \hat{h}_{t|t} &= \hat{h}_{t|t-1} + K_t \tilde{y}_t \\
   P_{t|t} &= (I - K_t H_t) P_{t|t-1}
   \end{aligned}29832

*Limitation:* Linear assumptions break down in nonlinear dynamics (fluid turbulence, biological rhythms, market regimes), driving the transition to neural deep state-space and transformer representations.

## 4. Deep Sequence Modeling & Neural Baselines

### 4.1 Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) and Gradient Vanishing
Standard RNNs maintain an evolving hidden representation:
29832h_t = \tanh(W_{hh} h_{t-1} + W_{xh} x_t + b_h)29832

Computing gradients across backpropagation through time (BPTT) requires the product of Jacobians:
29832\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h_t} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h_T} \prod_{k=t+1}^T \frac{\partial h_k}{\partial h_{k-1}} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial h_T} \prod_{k=t+1}^T W_{hh}^T \operatorname{diag}(1 - \tanh^2(a_k))29832

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:
29832\left\| \prod_{k=t+1}^T \frac{\partial h_k}{\partial h_{k-1}} \right\| \le \prod_{k=t+1}^T \|W_{hh}\| \cdot \|\operatorname{diag}(1 - \tanh^2(a_k))\|29832
If the spectral radius $\rho(W_{hh}) < 1$, the gradient norm decays exponentially as $\mathcal{O}(\gamma^{T-t})$ with $\gamma < 1$, resulting in the **vanishing gradient problem**.

### 4.2 LSTMs, GRUs, and Temporal Convolutions (TCN)
LSTMs (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997) circumvent vanishing gradients through an additive linear cell state highway:
29832c_t = f_t \odot c_{t-1} + i_t \odot \tilde{c}_t, \quad \frac{\partial c_t}{\partial c_{t-1}} = f_t29832
When the forget gate  \approx 1$, error gradients flow backward across hundreds of timesteps without exponential decay.

### 4.3 DeepAR: Autoregressive Probabilistic Neural Forecasting
Salinas et al. (Amazon Research, 2019) introduced DeepAR, replacing point forecasts with parametric likelihood outputs $\theta = (\mu, \sigma)$:
29832\mathcal{L}(\Theta) = - \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{t=1}^{T_i} \log p(x_{i,t} \mid \theta(h_{i,t}))29832
DeepAR demonstrated that training a single shared recurrent network across thousands of related series outperformed isolated classical models by learning shared seasonal cross-series representations.

## 5. The Transformer Architecture & The Continuous Patching Revolution

### 5.1 Point-Wise Self-Attention
Vaswani et al. (2017) formulated multi-head attention:
29832\operatorname{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \operatorname{softmax}\left( \frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}} \right) V29832
For a sequence of length $, the computation of ^T$ requires an $\mathcal{O}(L^2)$ quadratic matrix multiplication in time and memory.

### 5.2 The Failure Modes of Point-Wise Time Series Transformers
Early attempts to apply transformers directly to time series (LogTrans, Informer, Autoformer) treated each single scalar point  \in \mathbb{R}$ as an individual token. This approach failed for two profound mathematical reasons:

1. **Point-Wise Semantic Poverty:** A single scalar $ carries virtually zero semantic context. Unlike a word token in NLP (which carries rich syntactic and semantic information), a number like .2$ could represent ambient temperature, stock price, or voltage. The semantic meaning exists only in local trends, slopes, and curvature over a window.
2. **Quadratic Memory Collapse:** Capturing long lookback horizons ( = 512$ or $) caused memory consumption to explode quadratically while attention weights became uniformly diffused over noisy single points.

### 5.3 The PatchTST Breakthrough: Continuous Patching
Nie et al. (ICLR 2023) revolutionized time series transformers by introducing **Sub-Series Patching** (PatchTST):

#### Mathematical Formulation of Patching:
Given a lookback series  \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times L}$, extract patches of length $ with stride $:
29832p_i = x_{(i-1)S : (i-1)S + P} \in \mathbb{R}^P, \quad i \in \{1, 2, \dots, N\}29832
where the effective sequence length $ is:
29832N = \left\lfloor \frac{L - P}{S} \right\rfloor + 129832

Each continuous patch $ is linearly projected into the model dimension {\text{model}}$:
29832z_i = W_e p_i + b_e \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}}}, \quad W_e \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times P}29832

#### The Quadratic Speedup Factor:
Attention is computed across $ patch tokens rather than $ points. For non-overlapping patches ( = P$):
29832N = \frac{L}{P} \implies \mathcal{O}(N^2) = \mathcal{O}\left( \left(\frac{L}{P}\right)^2 \right) = \frac{1}{P^2} \mathcal{O}(L^2)29832
For a standard patch size of  = 16$ or  = 32$, this delivers a **\times$ to \times$ reduction** in attention complexity, allowing models to attend across immense lookback windows ( > 2048$) with crisp, localized temporal tokens.

### 5.4 Channel Independence vs Channel Mixing
PatchTST established the counter-intuitive principle of **Channel Independence (CI)**: each variable in a multivariate time series is tokenized independently through identical shared weights. CI prevents overfitting to spurious inter-channel correlations and allows one single trained model to forecast series with arbitrary channel counts.

### 5.5 Reversible Instance Normalization (RevIN)
To handle non-stationary distribution shift across test series, RevIN (Kim et al., ICLR 2022) normalizes the input patches and restores statistics after decoding:
29832\tilde{x}_t = \frac{x_t - \mu_x}{\sigma_x + \epsilon}, \quad \hat{y}_t = \hat{\tilde{y}}_t \cdot (\sigma_x + \epsilon) + \mu_x29832

### 6.1 Google Research Breakthrough: TimesFM-ICF (ICML 2025)
At ICML 2025, Rajat Sen and Yichen Zhou (Google Research) presented *"In-Context Fine-Tuning for Time-Series Foundation Models"*, fundamentally transforming how foundation models absorb domain-specific cues.

#### The Prompting Formulation
In standard zero-shot forecasting, the model receives only the target series history ^{\text{hist}}$:
29832\hat{y}^{\text{target}} = f_\theta(x^{\text{hist}})29832

In TimesFM-ICF, the model is prompted with $ related in-context examples $\{ (e_k^{\text{hist}}, e_k^{\text{future}}) \}_{k=1}^K$ preceding the target series:
29832\mathcal{S} = [ e_1^{\text{hist}} \circ e_1^{\text{future}} \circ \mathbf{v}_{\text{[SEP]}} \circ e_2^{\text{hist}} \circ e_2^{\text{future}} \circ \mathbf{v}_{\text{[SEP]}} \dots \circ \mathbf{v}_{\text{[SEP]}} \circ x^{\text{hist}} ]29832

#### The Common Separator Token $\mathbf{v}_{\text{[SEP]}}$
If multiple series are concatenated naïvely without boundary indicators, the multi-head self-attention mechanism bleeds level offsets across examples, causing severe prediction spikes (+8.5% error).

Google introduced a learnable token embedding vector:
29832\mathbf{v}_{\text{[SEP]}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}}}29832
When attention heads encounter $\mathbf{v}_{\text{[SEP]}}$, the causal mask isolates the raw mean amplitude of Example $ while allowing the query token in the Target Series to borrow seasonal frequencies, phase shifts, and trend dynamics.

#### Empirical Findings Across 23 Benchmark Datasets:
- **Baseline TimesFM (Zero-Shot):** 1.000 (Normalized MASE).
- **TimesFM-FT (Supervised Fine-Tuning):** 0.932 (6.8% improvement, but requires hundreds of backward passes and GPU memory).
- **TimesFM-ICF (In-Context Few-Shot, =3$):** **0.934 (6.6% improvement, achieving SFT accuracy with 0 gradient steps at inference time!)**

## 7. Dynamical Systems Grounding: The WAM Scientific Bridge

To rigorously verify whether a foundation model learns the true physical invariants of a time series rather than superficial interpolation, research platforms utilize controlled dynamical systems.

### 7.1 Takens' Delay Embedding Theorem
F. Takens (1981) proved that the full multi-dimensional phase space of a deterministic dynamical system can be faithfully reconstructed from a single observed scalar sequence $.

#### Theorem 7.1 (Takens' Theorem)
Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a compact manifold of dimension $. Let $\Phi: \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{M}$ be a smooth diffeomorphism, and : \mathcal{M} \to \mathbb{R}$ a smooth measurement function. The delay reconstruction map $\Psi_{(\Phi, y)}: \mathcal{M} \to \mathbb{R}^m$ defined by:
29832\Psi_{(\Phi, y)}(s) = \Big( y(s), \; y(\Phi^{-\tau}(s)), \; y(\Phi^{-2\tau}(s)), \; \dots, \; y(\Phi^{-(m-1)\tau}(s)) \Big)^T29832
is an **embedding** (a smooth, injective immersion with a proper homeomorphous image) provided that:
29832m > 2d29832

*Relevance to Foundation Models:* Takens' theorem guarantees that feeding a lookback window of length  \ge m$ into a foundation model provides sufficient mathematical rank to invert and recover the underlying multi-dimensional chaotic attractor.

### 7.2 Attractor Invariants & The Kaplan-Yorke Dimension
The chaoticity and geometric complexity of an attractor are defined by its **Lyapunov Spectrum** $\{\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_m\}$, where $\lambda_1 > 0$ indicates sensitive dependence on initial conditions.

The effective fractal dimension of the chaotic attractor is given by the **Kaplan-Yorke (Lyapunov) Dimension**:
29832D_{KY} = j + \frac{\sum_{i=1}^j \lambda_i}{|\lambda_{j+1}|}29832
where $ is the largest integer such that $\sum_{i=1}^j \lambda_i \ge 0$.

### 7.3 Canonical Benchmark Systems
The Kairos / WAM research laboratory leverages three controlled physical dynamical testbeds:

1. **Multi-Population Kuramoto Phase Oscillators:**
   29832\frac{d\theta_i}{dt} = \omega_i + \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N K_{ij} \sin(\theta_j - \theta_i)29832
   Tests community phase clustering and synchronization transition forecasting.

2. **Stuart–Landau Limit Cycle Networks:**
   29832\frac{dz_i}{dt} = (\lambda_i + i\omega_i)z_i - |z_i|^2 z_i + \sum_{j=1}^N C_{ij}(z_j - z_i)29832
   Tests bifurcation prediction and amplitude death boundaries.

3. **Kuramoto–Sivashinsky PDE (Spatiotemporal Chaos):**
   29832u_t + u u_x + u_{xx} + u_{xxxx} = 029832
   Exhibits intense chaotic turbulence with an underlying low-dimensional attractor ({KY} \approx 5\text{--}10$). Successfully forecasting KS verifies that a foundation model has recovered the low-dimensional invariant manifold.
